# Layered illustration and responsive sound

Read for art-directed illustration, strong reference matching, tactile motion, or rich
sound. Preserve the brief's actual medium instead of defaulting to a text-slide renderer.

## Match the visual mechanism

- Choose a coherent material and visual vocabulary. For a papercraft film, use actual
  textured assets, cut edges, overlap, shadows, printed marks, and restrained line work.
- Separate movable pieces: hero object, petals/parts, messengers, foreground accents,
  paths, and background. A single illustration slowly zooming behind text is not the
  same effect as independently animated layers.
- Generate original raster assets through an available image tool when warranted;
  obtain genuine alpha for cutouts, inspect edges and unwanted halos, and retain prompts.
  Use code-native vectors when shapes and exact geometry better fit the brief.
- Make transformation visible: assemble, unfold, connect, interrupt, repair, hand off,
  and reveal. Give important actions anticipation, contact, and settling time.
- Carry visual objects across scenes. Prefer motivated wipes, match movement, or an
  iris following the hero over unrelated decorative transitions.
- Reduce explanatory copy when motion carries the idea. Keep evidence labels and the
  final next action readable; expressive animation never excuses untrue claims.

## Compose sound around actions

- Create a cue sheet: event ID, exact time, visual cause, sound family, level, duration,
  and stereo position. Derive picture and sound from the same event/timeline source.
- Use distinct, coherent materials: soft paper friction/folds, rounded contact clicks,
  air movement for travel, tension for a break, and a clear resolution for recovery.
  Do not label synthesized noise as recorded foley.
- Layer a composed music bed, tactile foreground effects, subtle environment, and
  optional approved narration. A repeating sine tone is a scratch track, not a rich mix.
- Let sound follow screen direction with restrained panning. Keep critical information
  understandable in mono and through the picture; headphones are not a requirement.
- Vary density. Duck or pause the bed at a meaningful interruption; allow the repair
  and final reveal to resolve. Avoid continuous maximum intensity or repetitive hits.
- Keep narration intelligible. Use original, licensed, or authorized sources and never
  clone the reference presenter. Without narration, captions must still explain the film.
- Measure the final encoded mix for loudness and true peak. Use a destination-appropriate
  target; preserve dynamics. Report actual measurements and listening-review limits.

## Actual interactivity and verification

When a preview helps review, offer play/pause, replay, mute, volume, and chapter jumps.
Optional control-feedback sound must honor mute/volume and begin only after user input.
Release temporary audio nodes and subscriptions; do not autoplay sound on page load.
Maintain playback time when changing aspect ratios, and verify metadata readiness
before seeking. Test clicks, mute, replay, seek, format changes, and unavailable media.
Say which interactions belong to the preview and which cues are baked into the MP4.
Keep a linear, independently playable export alongside the interactive preview.
