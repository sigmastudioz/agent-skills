# Feedback and learning

Read when establishing a video brief and after meaningful feedback or a reviewed
revision. Use this process to improve future decisions without accumulating a
project transcript or forcing every film into the same style.

## Before the next film

Read relevant project learning notes, if present, and select only lessons that fit
the audience, purpose and medium. Turn those lessons into concrete storyboard or
review decisions. The current request overrides past choices. A previous tool,
voice or visual motif is an option, not a universal default.

## Capture evidence after a review

Keep a concise learning record in the video project's existing notes, or a
`LEARNINGS.md` when there is no suitable record. For each meaningful lesson retain:

- Feedback: the user's actual words or an explicitly labeled paraphrase, with the
  reviewed version or artifact. Separate direct feedback from the agent's inference.
- Change: what changed and why it was expected to address that feedback.
- Outcome: what the user subsequently said, what was technically verified, and what
  remains unreviewed. Successful encoding does not establish creative approval.
- Scope: project preference, candidate general lesson, or supported reusable lesson.
- Next application: one decision or check that would prevent repeating the problem.

Link selected before/after artifacts or frames when they make the lesson clearer.
Retain only useful evidence; do not copy whole conversations, private paths,
credentials or customer details into the installed skill. If the revision has not
yet been reviewed, mark the lesson as a candidate rather than inventing success.
A later contradiction should narrow, revise or retire the lesson.

## Promote a useful lesson

Keep brand identity, exact durations, approved scripts, chosen presets, private
service locations and asset inventories in the project. Promote a general lesson
when direct feedback or a demonstrated failure supports it and it changes a future
decision. Record its limits; one approved revision is not a controlled comparison
proving that each individual technique caused the improvement.

During an authorized skill update, inspect existing guidance first. Refine the
closest relevant instruction or reference rather than appending a duplicate rule.
Keep this entry point short and place substantial medium-specific guidance in the
existing art-direction, motion/sound or shader references. Reuse scripts only when
they solve repeated, demonstrated needs; a learning record alone does not justify
a new framework. Validate the skill and check edited reference links after changes.
Report what was changed and any installation or write restriction that remains.

Reading these instructions does not grant new permission to change an installed
skill, purchase generations, publish, message others or schedule future work.
Capture project feedback within the authorized task; promote skill changes when
the user or governing instructions authorize them. Saving lessons updates reusable
instructions; it does not train the underlying model or create background learning.

## Starting lessons from a reviewed campaign revision

A 45-second campaign was criticized as dull and too presentational. A later edit
combined a continuous request journey, spatial object animation, generated human
motion and revised narration. The user called the result a good improvement.
That feedback supports keeping the following approaches available; it does not
establish that any single technique is necessary or sufficient.

| Trigger | Decision to carry forward | Limit / review evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A film feels like a presentation | Give the story a meaningful action or transformation that carries across shots. Define why each cut follows the previous one. | Check the storyboard for continuity and purpose; concise instructional slides may still fit another brief. See [art direction](art-direction.md) and [motion and sound](motion-and-sound.md). |
| The user asks for stronger visuals | Set a visual quality target with representative hook, mechanism and payoff frames plus a short motion study. Specify framing, depth, camera intent and transition before the long render. | Judge the motion study as well as stills; more effects or continuous camera movement alone do not establish improvement. |
| Human moments need subtle movement while object scenes need exact timing | Consider a hybrid of authorized generated footage and code-rendered objects or typography. Choose the medium per shot, retaining source trims and continuity anchors. | Generated footage is optional; inspect identity, hands, motion and continuity. Tool and generation authorization still apply. |
| Narration quality or delivery is uncertain | Discover an available authorized voice engine and audition a short representative passage before full synthesis. Check names, local expressions, numbers and pacing. | Keep the chosen voice in project notes. After a voice change, realign captions, rebuild ducking and verify the actual encoded audio; ASR is not proof of pronunciation or a listening review. |

The production details for a specific campaign remain with that campaign. Reuse
its reasoning and proven mechanics where relevant while creating a direction that
fits the next brief.
