# Teaching older adults

Practical defaults from adult-education and library tech-help practice. They are starting
assumptions to adjust after the first session, not research findings with fixed numbers.

## Session shape (90-120 minutes)

1. **Welcome and check-in (10 min):** what did you try since last time, what went wrong?
2. **One skill, demonstrated (10-15 min):** projected screen or document camera, slow, narrated
   ("I'm tapping the blue word Share, bottom left").
3. **Guided practice on the learner's own phone (25-35 min):** everyone does each step together;
   helpers circulate.
4. **Break (10 min).** Bathrooms, water, charging.
5. **Independent try (20-30 min):** learners repeat the task from the step card alone; helpers
   watch and only point.
6. **Recap and take-home card (10 min):** one sentence of what we learned, one thing to practise.

### Session one is different

Setup takes most of the first session. Plan roughly: sign-in and name tags with a printed form
code (15 min), a short intake per learner (phone model and system version, what they most want
to do, accessibility needs, languages), the pre-class confidence form read aloud on request
(15 min), then module 0 setup one-to-one with helpers (30-40 min) and only one short task,
such as making text bigger, practised by everyone. Leave the rest for session two.

Keep the room's pace to the group's slowest comfortable learner; offer a "go further" card for
fast learners rather than speeding up.

## Staffing

- **Helpers:** start with roughly one instructor or trained helper for every three to five learners
  in hands-on segments; adjust after seeing the group. Peer helpers (returning learners), library
  staff or vetted volunteers work well when briefed on [learner safety](learner-safety.md).
- **Group size:** small groups (about 8-12) leave room for individual help; larger rooms need more
  helpers, not a faster pace.
- **Language:** offer sessions or helpers in the group's languages where possible.

## Teaching moves that help

- Plain words, defined once and written on the board: "app", "icon", "link", "Wi-Fi", "account".
- Hands off: the helper points or describes; the learner taps. If a learner asks for help tapping
  (tremor, arthritis), the helper may tap non-sensitive steps with consent, never credentials,
  payments, health data or messages ([learner safety](learner-safety.md)).
- Repeat: the same task three times in one session beats three tasks once.
- Normalise mistakes: show a mistake on the projected screen and how to undo it.
- Separate iPhone and Android instructions; never make a learner translate one into the other.
- Avoid timed quizzes and public correction; praise specific progress.
- Write down where things are ("Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size"), because
  learners practise later without you.

## Accessibility settings to teach early

Exact menu names vary by maker and version; check on current devices.

| Need | iPhone (recent iOS) | Android (varies by maker) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Larger text | Settings > Display & Brightness > Text Size; Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Larger Text | Settings > Display (or Accessibility) > Font size / Display size |
| Bold text and contrast | Accessibility > Display & Text Size | Accessibility > Display or Visibility enhancements |
| Magnifier | Magnifier app or Accessibility shortcut | Accessibility > Magnification; camera zoom |
| Captions on calls and videos | Accessibility > Live Captions where supported | Accessibility > Live Caption where supported |
| Hearing aids | Accessibility > Hearing Devices (compatible aids) | Bluetooth or Accessibility > Hearing aids (compatible aids) |
| Reading aloud | Accessibility > Spoken Content | Accessibility > Select to Speak / Text-to-speech |
| Fewer accidental taps | Accessibility > Touch (Touch Accommodations, Haptic Touch) | Accessibility > Interaction (touch-and-hold delay) |

## Materials

- Step cards: one task per page, 16-18 pt or larger sans-serif text, high contrast, numbered
  steps with a cropped screenshot each, space for the learner's own notes. Separate iPhone and
  Android versions, dated, and reprinted when menus change.
- A glossary card, a "who to ask for help" card (library tech help, family, the class follow-up
  contact) and a scam-safety card.
- Large-print sign-in sheet (first name or initials, form code, session ticks), an intake card,
  name tags, practice QR codes, demo menu page, sample passes.

## Room checklist

Quiet room with good light and little glare, tables, chairs with arms, power strips within reach,
reliable Wi-Fi tested with the number of devices expected, projector or large screen, accessible
entrance and restrooms, parking or transit access, water.
