# Funding and partners

Every entry below is a **lead to verify**, not a promise of money. Programmes open, close, change
eligibility and get cut. When you use this list, open each programme's official page, record the
date you checked and what it said, and mark anything you could not confirm as unverified.

## About viral funding figures

Social-media pitches for this business model sometimes quote large annual figures that a city or
county will pay. Treat any such figure as the creator's unverified claim. Do not repeat it in a
proposal, price sheet, pitch or marketing. Price from your own costs ([cost sheet](pricing-and-costs.md))
and the partner's actual budget.

## Who pays, and how

| Payer | Typical route | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Municipality (senior services, recreation, library) | Small purchase or quote, a service contract, or a line in a programme budget | Who owns the budget; the purchasing agent's quote and contract rules |
| County office on aging / Area Agency on Aging | Older Americans Act funds (often Title III-B supportive services) and state funds flow through the county's area plan; services are often bought through periodic requests for proposals | The county's current area plan and procurement calendar |
| Public library or library system | Programme budget, Friends of the Library, library grants | Programme coordinator; whether outside instructors are paid or volunteer |
| Senior centers, senior housing, housing authorities | Resident-services or activity budgets | The resident-services coordinator |
| Health systems, health plans, community organisations | Community-benefit or member-engagement programmes | Their community-programmes office; no learner health data shared |
| Banks and credit unions | Community-investment and financial-education support | The community-relations or CRA officer; no product promotion in class |
| Community foundations and local service clubs | Small community grants | Eligibility, which often requires a nonprofit or government applicant |
| Learners | Low per-person fee or sliding scale | Keep a free or low-cost option; many free classes already exist |

## For-profit providers

Many grants are open only to nonprofits, governments or libraries. A for-profit provider usually:

1. Contracts with the grantee (city, county, library, nonprofit) as the paid instructor or vendor;
   or
2. Partners with a nonprofit that applies and subcontracts the teaching, with roles written down;
   or
3. Sells directly to venues (senior housing, community centers) or to learners.

Public contracts carry their own rules. In New Jersey, ask the purchasing agent about quote and
bid thresholds, the business registration certificate, pay-to-play disclosures, insurance
certificates, affirmative-action paperwork (the Employee Information Report, form AA-302, or an
equivalent) and any background-check requirement before quoting. These rules change; do not
quote thresholds from memory.

## Sponsors

A sponsor may fund seats, loaner phones, printing or venue costs. Put the terms in writing:
- Credit on flyers, the welcome slide and the final report.
- Optional brochures on a side table, or a separate, clearly labelled event outside class time,
  if the venue partner allows it.
- No sales talk, sign-ups or referral fees during sessions.
- No sharing of learner names or contacts.
- The curriculum stays brand-neutral and teaches free built-in features first.

A sponsor that insists on pitching inside a session is one to decline.

## Partner pitch checklist

- The problem in the partner's words (residents who cannot use app-only parking, QR menus, patient
  portals; scam losses; isolation).
- What learners will be able to do, the module list and session shape.
- Safety boundaries ([learner safety](learner-safety.md)) and data handling.
- Measurement plan and the one-page report they will receive ([outcomes](outcomes.md)).
- Price per cohort and what is included ([cost sheet](pricing-and-costs.md)).
- Pilot first: one cohort, then a review before a larger contract.

For writing a grant or RFP response, use the small-business grant-rfp-writer skill if installed;
for a quote or proposal, the proposal-builder skill. Neither this skill nor those skills submits
anything or contacts funders on the provider's behalf.
