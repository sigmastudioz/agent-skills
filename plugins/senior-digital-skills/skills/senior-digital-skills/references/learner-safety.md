# Learner safety and boundaries

Brief every instructor and helper on these before the first session, in writing. Older learners
are frequent scam targets, and a class that asks people to hand over their phone teaches the
exact habit scammers exploit.

## Never

- Ask for, type, read aloud, write down, photograph or store a learner's password, PIN, passcode,
  verification code, card or bank number, ID, Social Security or Medicare number.
- Log in, sign up, buy, pay, transfer money, accept terms or change account settings for a learner.
- Sign anyone into a personal account on a loaner phone, or hand a loaner on without signing out
  and resetting it.
- Take a learner's phone out of their sight, or install apps, profiles or remote-access tools on it.
- Look through photos, messages, email or health records, or let others see them.
- Promote or sell devices, phone plans, apps or services to learners, or take referral fees.
- Photograph or record learners or their screens without separate, written, opt-in consent.

## Instead

- **Point, don't take.** The learner holds the phone and taps; the helper points or describes. If
  the learner asks, a helper may tap a non-sensitive step (opening Settings, scrolling) with the
  phone staying in the learner's hands and view; never credentials, payments, health data or messages.
- **Privacy screen moments.** When a learner types a password or code, helpers look away and say so.
- **Forgotten passwords.** Show where the official "forgot password" option is; the learner resets
  it themselves, now or at home with someone they trust. Do not guess or invent passwords for them.
- **Demo accounts.** Use the instructor's own test accounts, demo menus and sample passes for
  anything involving money, health or identity.
- **Help in the shared room.** Avoid one-to-one help out of others' sight.

## Data the class collects

Collect only what the class needs: first name or initials for name tags, sign-in for attendance,
an optional contact for class updates, and anonymous survey answers. Keep sign-in sheets locked
or shredded after the reporting period agreed with the partner. Do not share learner lists with
sponsors or marketers. If a partner requires named data, get written consent and follow the
partner's own data policy.

## If something goes wrong

- **A learner reveals a scam or loss in progress:** stop the exercise, help them call their bank
  or card issuer on the number printed on their card, and point to official fraud reporting, the US
  Department of Justice National Elder Fraud Hotline (833-FRAUD-11; scammers also impersonate it,
  so dial it directly) and local police. If a remote-access app was installed at a scammer's
  request, help the learner turn on airplane mode and contact their bank and a trusted repair
  service. Do not take over their accounts.
- **Concern about abuse, neglect or exploitation of a vulnerable adult:** follow the partner
  venue's procedure and the local adult protective services route (in New Jersey, county adult
  protective services providers; check the current contact on the state's official site).
- **A device problem the class cannot fix:** refer to the carrier, maker or library tech help.
  Do not factory-reset, root or "clean up" a learner's phone.
- Write a short incident note (what happened, what was done, no sensitive details) and tell the
  partner contact.

## Instructor readiness

Partners may require background checks, insurance certificates and a signed code of conduct; ask
early. Keep the venue's emergency contacts, first-aid location and exit plan on hand.
